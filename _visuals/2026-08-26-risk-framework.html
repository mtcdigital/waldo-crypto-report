🧬 The Risk Framework

How the $1M recommendation decides what each risk tier holds — synthesized from the brothers' Aug 25 call. Two layers: every coin gets a risk tag from live data, and every tier blends all four silos. Only the mix changes.

Layer 1 — every coin gets a risk tag (live data, not opinions)

Data source: CoinGecko — market-cap rank, the "Meme" category flag, and live market cap. Refreshed weekly, cached, with a safe fallback if the API is rate-limited. Rules fire in this order:

Inside each silo, coins are weighted by √(live market cap) — bigger coin, bigger slice, but the giants don't drown everything. The Twitter Scraper's job upstream is to surface new candidates for the universe; CoinGecko then classifies them by the same rules, no hand-tagging.

Layer 2 — every tier blends all four silos (the blend matrix)

Your consensus numbers. No silo is ever exclusive: high risk keeps a Bitcoin sliver, medium keeps a taste of the memes — the "one silo, parse it out" idea from the call.

Why it works this way (the honest reasoning)

No source publishes risk-tiered allocations. Only CCI comes close, and they're foundationally conservative (their "high risk" still holds ~40% BTC). So: the sources feed what to buy and when (scores, calls, scraper candidates); the blend matrix is the family's own rule set, written down once, applied consistently — instead of re-prompting an AI's mood every week.
The tags are data, the matrix is policy. Facts (rank, meme flag, market cap) come from CoinGecko and update themselves. The judgment calls (60/30/10 splits, return targets) are yours — each one is a single number in the config, changeable in one line when the brothers want to re-vote.
Return targets are framing, not promises. "High risk targets ~3.5–5×" describes the kind of outcome the mix is built to chase, and what it can lose chasing it.
